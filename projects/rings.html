Research

Reconfigurable Rings

Predicting elastic rod buckling behavior to inform reconfigurable structure design.

TimelineWinter 2025 – Summer 2025
LabSoft Intelligent Materials Laboratory (PI: Prof. Renee Zhao)
CollaboratorsDr. Jeseung Lee (Zhao Lab), Prof. Wei Chen's Lab (Northwestern University)
LinksZhao Lab

Objective

Understand and predict a single rod's buckling behavior as a function of length, radius, height, and width. Understanding rods at this component level helps inform potential system designs and applications.

Background

The overall goal of the project was to understand the capabilities of reconfigurable rings and how their stabilities and instabilities can be useful in reconfigurable structures. When these rods are compressed, many experience "snapping" — sudden buckling elastic rod movements during both loading and unloading. We wanted to predict specific buckling behavior based on ring parameters.

My Role & Contribution

I modeled, 3D printed, and tested over 100 different rod configurations with varying cross-section dimensions, lengths, and radii.

Outcome

Our data points helped develop software for predicting buckling behavior, specifically singling out snap-through behavior. This data and prediction will inform the creation of new reconfigurable structures.

Future Work
