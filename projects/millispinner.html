Research

Magnetic Milli-Spinner

Optimizing a magnetically actuated milli-scale robot for minimally invasive endovascular intervention.

TimelineSummer 2025 – present
LabSoft Intelligent Materials Laboratory (PI: Prof. Renee Zhao)
CollaboratorsDr. Lu, Zhao Lab, Stanford Dept. of Mechanical Engineering
LinksZhao Lab · Publication (Advanced Materials)

Objective

Develop a more optimized magnetic spinning robot for minimally invasive endovascular surgery — building on the Zhao Lab's 2024 magnetic milli-spinner to make the robot more effective, stable, and fast.

My Role & Contribution

I 3D modeled, printed, and tested over 200 spinner designs with varying geometric parameters, each with different stability and speed characteristics. For reliable navigation inside the human body, the spinner needs to be able to swim against blood flow, so I optimized robot geometry through computational fluid dynamics (CFD), rapid prototyping, and experimental validation — achieving a 5× improvement in swimming speed. I presented this work on the Magnetic Milli-Spinner at the Bay Area Robotics Symposium.

Outcome

We set a new record for the fastest untethered medical device — improving from 29 cm/s in the 2024 paper to 55 cm/s. This work is documented in Optimization of Magnetic Milli-Spinner for Robotic Endovascular Intervention, Lu, Higgins, Bernardo, Zhao (2026).

Future Work

Eventually, this technology is intended to go to market, improving the efficiency of endovascular intervention worldwide.
